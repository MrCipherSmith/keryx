### Timing tests: are they flaky on a loaded CI runner?
- `expectLinearTime` (ledger.test.ts:79-93) is not flaky. Each size takes the best of three runs after a warm-up. The bound `large < 12·small + 25 ms` is dominated by the additive 25 ms, while the 200k-char work is a single hand-written scan, about a millisecond. A runner ten times slower scales `small` too, so the threshold rises with it. The test still fails a real regression: a backtracking pattern on 200k chars costs seconds. A mild superlinear cost would pass. That is acceptable for its purpose.
- The fileHash same-size rewrite (completion-gate.test.ts:671-673) is likely to flake on Linux (LG-R5-002, minor). Coarse inode timestamps leave the stat key unchanged, and the test bypasses the racy window that guards production.
- review-pr-base.test.ts runs six sequential cold `bun run` children that each import review.ts, under CI's 5 s default timeout. Its siblings use 30-60 s (LG-R5-003, minor).
- map-bounded.test.ts is deterministic: the peak equals the limit by construction, and the order follows the input index.
